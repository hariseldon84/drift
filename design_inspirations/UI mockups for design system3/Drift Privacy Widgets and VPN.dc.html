Tinnitus: symptoms, causes, and when to see a doctor
Reviewed by Dr. Meera Krishnan, MD · Updated 11 February 2026
Tinnitus is the perception of sound when no external sound is present — most often a ringing, but also described as buzzing, hissing, or clicking. It affects an estimated 15 percent of adults, and for roughly one in five of those it is persistent enough to interfere with sleep or concentration.
Prolonged exposure to loud sound is the most common identifiable cause. Volume above 85 decibels sustained over time damages the hair cells of the inner ear, and that damage does not reverse.
